## Course Description

This course is a survey of emerging topics in healthcare human-systems engineering. The course uses the Design for X (DfX) methodology to discuss a wide range of contexts, technologies, and population dependent criteria (X's) that must be considered in the design of a safe and usable healthcare ecosystem. Applications of Human Factors delves into critical and emergent issues in healthcare and patient safety and how the field of human factors and ergonomics (HFE) play a role in this domain. Typical design and operational problems in healthcare domains, as well as their HFE solutions, will be highlighted. Students will apply HFE principles to design problems and a research project will allow them to explore in depth a relevant HFE topic of interest.

## Course Learning Outcomes

1. The students will be able to describe the role and scope of human factors/ergonomics in healthcare.
2. The students will be able to apply human factors engineering methods to analysis, design, implementation, and evaluation of healthcare systems.
3. The students will be able to critically examine several emerging topics related to HF/E in healthcare and their implications and consequences as related to productivity, safety, wellness, and satisfaction in healthcare settings.
4. The students will be able to exhibit effective teamwork and sharpen their communication skills.

### Research report:

Undergraduate students will conduct an original scoping or narrative review. Graduate student will work individually to conduct an original study and will generate a research paper on a topic relevant to the course, due near the end of the semester. A research report proposal will be due around the 3rd week of class, so that we can judge the relevance and depth of the topic and provide advice/guidance. Further details on this report will be given early in the semester.

### Homework assignment:

There will be approximately three homework assignments, designed to give you practice in applying human factors methods learned in the course. Some but not all of the assignments may be graded (the instructor will specify when they are graded). Assignments will be posted on Canvas and announced in lecture and/or via email. Due dates for each exam will be given when they are issued but will generally be within 1 week. Unless otherwise specified, completed assignments should be submitted on Canvas.

### Reflections:

Student will work on five assignments reflecting on the topic of the week and researching healthcare applications of the concepts discussed.

### Practicum:

Student will work in teams of 3 to prepare and deliver a detailed case study on one of the course topics. The lecture should cover background research and an in-depth case study illustrating the application of HF/E to their topic of choice.
