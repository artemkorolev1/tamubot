# 2 GENERAL INFORMATION

## 2.1 COURSE DESCRIPTION

Introduces the principles of lean thinking in modern manufacturing systems; philosophical, managerial and organizational requirements studied; lean manufacturing quantitative modeling methodologies, lean manufacturing cell design and case study analysis.

2.2 PREREQUISITES

ISEN 609 or by approval of instructor.

## 2.3 LEARNING OBJECTIVES

A lean production system (LPS) is one that generates goods and services with minimal buffering of products and resources. Systems engineering provides a compendium of methods and models to represent and characterize the complex nature of production systems and their interdependencies. The course is architected to expose the students to the knowledge, skills, and experiences (KSEs) of lean engineering supported by systems thinking.

The core principles of lean production systems are summarized as value, value stream, flow, control, and perfection. The lectures and class discussions have been organized around these 5-core principles. Like most systems, the LPS requires maintenance or it decays. The principle of perfection alludes to the never ending drive towards the 'lean production system upon a hill' - that is, true single-piece flow and quantity control enabled through quality control. ## 2.4 EXPECTATIONS

This is a course in Lean Engineering and students will not only learn how to think in terms of Lean but will also have a 'hands on' experience by working on practical exercises. Students will be placed in teams of 3-4 students, and each team will work the exercise to reinforce lean concepts. The teams will apply lean engineering to analyze and address manufacturing issues.
