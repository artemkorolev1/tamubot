## Course Description

Introduction of convex geometry with a focus on its use in advanced optimization modeling and algorithms. On the theory side, this course serves as a first graduate level course covering several basic aspects of convex geometry, including convexity in topological vector spaces, volumes of convex bodies, lattice and polyhedral geometry, and convex algebraic geometry. On the application side, this course aims at exposing to students several research frontiers related to robust, stochastic, integer, and polynomial optimization in operations research and data science.

## Tentative course outline

- Convexity in topological vector spaces

- -convex sets and hyperplane separation

- -convex functions and duality in optimization

- -application: reformulations of distributionally robust optimization

- Convex bodies and volumes

- -polytopes and ellipsoids

- -application: introduction to complexity theory for optimization

- -Brunn-Minkowski inequality and measure concentration

- -application: convex optimization for exact recovery

- Polyhedra and integer points

- -integer lattice and Minkowski's theorem

- -application: integer linear optimization in fixed dimensions

- -integer points in polyhedral cones

- -application: some primal methods for integer optimization

- Sums of squares and nonnegativity

- -sums of squares on algebraic and semi-algebraic sets

- -moment and truncated moment problems

- -application: Lasserre-Parrilo hierarchy for polynomial optimization

## Prerequisites

Familiarity with proof-based undergraduate-level linear algebra and real analysis is required. Experience with a graduate-level optimization course, such as ISEN 622/623 or MATH 651/652, is recommended.

Homework assignments : students can work on any 10 problems out of a given set of exercise problems in the semester. Each problem will be graded on a 5-point scale: 5 for complete and valid arguments, 3/4 for reasonable attempts but with possible flaws or gaps, and 0-2 for missing submission or insufficient effort. Students will have the chance to revise and resubmit their works before the final report day (December 5 th ).

Collaboration and discussion on homework is allowed in this class. However, a student must write their own answers and cite any individuals they work with or resources they use, including anything generated by artificial intelligence. Failure to give credit when presenting someone else's work is plagiarism, which violates academic integrity and will result in an F* in the course.

Course project : each student is expected to complete a project which investigates a theoretical topic or addresses a particular application relevant to this course. Potential topics and references will be given, but the students are encouraged to find their own topic of interest. In particular,

- a 1-page project proposal is due by October 29 th (tentatively), in which the student should propose a topic of interest, together with adequate background, motivation, and literature review;

- towards the end of the semester, each student is expected to present their project to others in scheduled class times;

- a final report for the project, in the format of a short conference paper (max. 5 pages excluding appendices), will be due tentatively December 5 th .

Students are welcome to meet with the instructor for suggestions and comments on the project throughout the semester. If the project involves numerical experiments, then for reproducibility reasons, it is suggested that the student use open-source tools and include a link to all of the relevant code/data in their final report.

## Plagiarism Policy

According to the Texas A&M University Definitions of Academic Misconduct, plagiarism is the appropriation of another person's ideas, processes, results or words without giving appropriate credit ( https://aggiehonor.tamu.edu ). AI text generators (ChatGPT, Google Bard, etc.) should not be used for any work for this class without explicit permission of the instructor and appropriate attribution. This includes, but is not limited to:
